# Starlight

Date: January 31, 2024
Movement: Romanticism
Sub-Theme One: Cosmic
Theme: Nature & Cosmos

The sky at night is less dark than I once thought,
Full of distant life and love,
Laughter and lessons,
Pain and purpose,
Suffering and spite.

Yet still, less dark than I once thought,
More blue than black,
Spotted by tiny speckles of white that glisten like diamonds held to the retina,
Like spires of selenite seen from a mile away.

Tell me again, that those specks of spectres hold masses larger than ours,
That our Sun is but a child to their giant,
A David to their Goliath with no fight to win and no people to deliver.

Tell me why I should believe the words of men like I,
Who conjure myths as easily as witches do potions; warlocks do spells; helpless do screams.

If those specks of spectres do hold stardust of unfathomable scale: what am I to an ant? What is he to me, am I a black hole in its universe? A dying of the light?

One day I will peer into the verse, and reach these specks of spectres, that glimmer like ghosts of solar systems at large; crying in light, not tears: begging to be found by sentience above our own.

One day,
The specks of spectres may reveal themselves,
One day.
